Overview
This document captures the finalized domain model for Postcard Travel Club's content architecture. Eight diagrams cover the full model: the content hierarchy, the geo tier, classification, the actor/user layer, the generic Circle relationship entity, Memory/Analytics, and a consolidated master overview. Each diagram is on its own page, followed by a full field-level schema section for every entity involved.
The model follows one governing principle: every classification, relationship, and grouping is a relation or join table, not a hardcoded column, so new dimensions are additive — no restructuring required.
Figure 1. Collection Hierarchy
The core content hierarchy. CollectionType is the category (Properties, Restaurants, Events, Shopping, Destination Expert). Collection is one specific instance — a named Property, Restaurant, Event, or Shop. Postcard is the atomic content unit.
Sub-type classification (Boutique Stays, Signature Experiences, Cuisine) is handled entirely through FacetAssignment (Figure 4), not a separate Category entity — an earlier draft of this model had a dedicated Category/CollectionCategory pair that turned out to duplicate exactly what FacetType/FacetValue already does, so it was folded in rather than kept as a second mechanism doing the same job.
* CollectionType → Collection is a straightforward one-to-many.
* Collection → Postcard is nullable: only CollectionTypes with has_dedicated_collection = true (Properties, Destination Expert) wrap their postcards in a Collection. Restaurants/Events/Shopping postcards stand alone as their own entry, with no Collection layer above them.
* FacetAssignment applies polymorphically to Collection (shown here) or directly to Postcard (shown in Figure 4) — the same mechanism covers both.

Figure 2. Subcollection & CollectionCluster
Subcollection (Journey) and CollectionCluster (City Guide, Partner Affiliation) both build on top of the Figure 1 hierarchy rather than replacing it.
A Subcollection always belongs to exactly one Collection — e.g. a Journey is a curated, ordered subset of postcards pulled from one specific Property's own postcards, never mixed across properties (multi-property journeys remain a possible future extension, not built now).
* SubcollectionPostcard is an ordered join carrying sequence_order — a manually chosen display position (Day 1, Day 2…), not a calendar date. Real calendar dates are assigned later, per member, when a Journey is actually booked.
* CollectionCluster groups entries polymorphically via CollectionClusterEntry, which can point at either a Collection (a Property, if one ever joins a cluster) or a Postcard directly (the common case for Restaurants/Events/Shopping, since they have no Collection wrapper of their own).
* Membership in a cluster is explicit editorial curation, not an automatic filter — consistent with the platform's curation-over-volume principle.

Figure 3. Geo Hierarchy
A four-tier location hierarchy: Country → Region → City → Locality. This exists specifically to prevent name collisions that caused real problems in the legacy system — city names repeating across different countries, and locality/neighborhood names repeating across different cities (e.g. “Indira Nagar” exists in both Mumbai and Bangalore).
* Each tier is uniquely scoped to its parent, not globally unique: Region is unique by (name, country_id), City by (name, region_id), Locality by (name, city_id).
* This tier is reused everywhere location matters — Postcard, Collection, CollectionCluster, User, and Memory all reference it directly rather than storing free-text location strings.

Figure 4. Classification (Facets & Tags)
FacetType/FacetValue/FacetAssignment is the single classification mechanism for the whole platform — it replaced two originally hardcoded fields (category and environment) that broke the moment a second CollectionType (Events) needed different classification dimensions than the one they were designed for (Properties), and it also absorbed a separately-designed Category/CollectionCategory pair once that turned out to be the same pattern applied a second time.
A FacetType (“Property Type”, “Experience Theme”, “Cuisine”) can be scoped to a specific CollectionType and can allow either single or multiple values. Boutique Stays and Signature Experiences are FacetValues under “Property Type”; Indian and Italian are FacetValues under “Cuisine”. Adding a new classification dimension in the future is just a new FacetType row — no schema change.
* FacetAssignment is polymorphic, applying to either a Collection or a Postcard, matching the same postcard-as-entry split described in Figure 1.
* Tag remains its own separate, simpler mechanism — granular, always-multi-select, postcard-level features (stargazing, infinity pool) that sit a level below whatever a Facet classifies.

Figure 4 — FacetType, FacetValue, FacetAssignment, and Tag — the single classification mechanism, covering both sub-typing and thematic tagging.
Figure 5. Actors: User, UserType, UserRole, Company
A single unified User table (not split by Member/Staff/Partner) matches how the platform already works and preserves identity continuity when someone's role changes — a Member becoming a Destination Expert, for example, without migrating their history to a different table.
UserRole replaces a single user_type_id column, because one person can hold more than one role — a Partner who is also a personal Member, or a Partner managing listings for two different Companies.
* UserRole.company_id is nullable and only populated for partner-type roles, and can differ across multiple roles held by the same person.
* UserRole.manager_user_id is a self-referential link supporting an org-chart hierarchy (an Editor's role reports to an Editor-in-Chief's role), recursive by nature since it just points back at User.

Figure 6. Circle — the universal relationship layer
Circle is the single mechanism for every “a User relates to X” case on the platform. It replaces six separate, duplicated legacy entities (Bookmark, FollowAlbum, FollowCompany, FollowAffiliate, FollowTag, FollowCityGuide) plus a standalone User-follows-User entity — all of which turned out to be the same shape repeated per type.
owned_type is polymorphic across every ownable/followable entity in the system, including another User (following a person, not just content).
* relationship distinguishes what kind of connection this is: author and assigned_staff cover content stewardship — previously separate columns duplicated across every content entity; owner covers business/commercial ownership (a Partner's properties, queryable directly by user_id); bookmark and booked cover personal member engagement, with booked additionally carrying source_enquiry_id and sequence_date for a booked Journey's personal fork.
* This single table, properly indexed on (user_id, relationship, owned_type), answers every variant of “show me everything connected to this person” in one place.

Figure 7. Memory & Persona/Analytics
Memory is the member's personal narrative layer — photos, notes, and travel companions attached to a specific bookmarked Postcard, or standing alone if it doesn't map to any curated content at all. It is deliberately distinct from Circle's booked relationship: Circle tracks the structural fact of what was booked and when; Memory carries the personal story on top of that.
UserEvent and UserPersona formalize behavioral tracking that was previously just a vague “feeds personalization” line — now with real, queryable schema.
* Memory.postcard_id is nullable — only a Postcard can be the subject, since a Memory is about a specific personal experience, not an abstract container.
* UserEvent is the raw, append-only behavioral log. UserPersona is the periodically recomputed, derived profile — an ML embedding plus scalar signals like price_affinity.
* UserPersonaTag is a proper weighted join, not a JSON blob, so it can be queried in both directions: a member's own top interests, and every member interested in a given tag for targeting.

Figure 8. Master Overview — All Entities & Relationships
A single consolidated view of every entity across the seven preceding diagrams, grouped into the same six clusters plus the Circle relationship hub. This is a relationship map, not a substitute for the detailed figures — refer back to Figures 1–7 and the Entity Schemas section for field-level detail.
* Geo Hierarchy is shown as its own self-contained chain (Country → Region → City → Locality); its many inbound references from Postcard, Collection, CollectionCluster, User, and Memory are omitted here to keep the diagram legible — see the individual figures for those connections.
* Circle sits at the center as the universal relationship hub between User and everything ownable/followable (Postcard, Collection, Subcollection, CollectionCluster, Tag, Company, and User itself).
* The two dense clusters — Content Hierarchy and Subcollection & Cluster — are where most of the platform's actual browsing and curation logic lives; Classification and Memory & Analytics layer on top of them without altering their shape.
